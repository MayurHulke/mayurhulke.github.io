<span class="act-head">Beginnings</span>

![Hiro and Tadashi from Big Hero 6](/img/big-hero-six.gif)

My love for robots came from stories, films and books all mixed together. *I, Robot*, *Big Hero 6*, Andy Weir's *Project Hail Mary*, and Isaac Asimov's robot collection, which I worked my way through. Asimov shaped how I think about machines more than any film has.

At some point I stopped just watching robots on screen and started trying to build them. I got into robotics competitions pretty early and ended up winning **30+ of them at a national level** 🏆. That was probably the moment I realised I'd spend my life around robots somehow.

<span class="act-head">Founding</span>

![With the intelligent bionic leg prototype](/img/mayur-leg.jpg)

I studied **Advanced Robotics & Artificial Intelligence** at the **University of Bristol** and spent years at the [**Bristol Robotics Laboratory**](https://www.bristolroboticslab.com/), one of the biggest robotics labs in the UK, working on prosthetics research.

Then the research turned into a company. I founded **Chisel Robotics** and ran it for five years, building an **intelligent bionic leg** for people with lower-limb amputation: a socket that understands fit, gait and movement in real time, with feedback under **50 ms**. We validated it across **four NHS trials** and built performance legs for Paralympic athletes, including a **Rio Paralympic gold medallist** 🥇. It was backed by **Innovate UK**, grew out of **NHS** research partnerships, and won a place at **SETsquared**, the world's #1 university business incubator.

That changed how I thought about robotics. The best robots don't always look like sci-fi. Sometimes they just help someone get a piece of their life back.

<span class="act-head">Production</span>

I also spent years shipping computer vision into places where a bad prediction costs real money. At **Q-Free** I built vehicle detection, classification and tracking for UK and US motorways, running on **NVIDIA Jetson and Xavier** hardware at the roadside, hitting **99.7% detection accuracy at 65+ FPS** across ten deployed units. At **BCA** I led the vision systems for automated vehicle inspection, cutting inspection from around four hours to **under 90 minutes** per car.

Demos are easy. Systems that run every day, on real hardware, in bad weather, with nobody watching, are not. That's the lesson I've carried into everything since.

<span class="act-head">Perception</span>

<div class="act-art act-art--sketch"><img src="/img/storyboard/sketch-6-factories-that-see.svg" alt="A factory connected to a network of 60+ sites"></div>

If robots are going to act in the world, they first need to understand what's happening around them. I joined **Almetra** (previously Deltia) as a **Staff Computer Vision Engineer** and built a **7B vision-language model** for **spatio-temporal action segmentation**. Show it a video and it breaks down what's happening, action by action, over short and long time horizons.

We deployed it across **60+ factory sites** for **ABB**, **Bosch**, **Continental**, **Thermo Fisher**, **Viessmann Climate Solutions**, **Grundfos** and **Siemens Energy**, helping teams see patterns on the factory floor that were previously invisible.

<span class="act-head">Physical AI</span>

![Our Franka Research 3 Duo and Universal Robots cell at Almetra](/img/franka-lab.jpg)

In January 2026 I moved across to lead robotics. As **Robotics Lead at [Almetra](https://www.almetra.ai/)**, I'm building the robotics division from the ground up alongside our Co-founder & CTO, and architecting the **perception-to-action stack**, turning that understanding of real manufacturing work into policies that can plan and execute physical tasks.

I also lead our technical collaborations with frontier robotics partners: the **Gemini Robotics Accelerator** with [**Google DeepMind**](https://blog.google/topics/google-europe/powering-the-future-of-robotics-in-europe/?utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=&utm_content=), and the [**MassRobotics Physical AI Fellowship**](https://www.massrobotics.org/massrobotics-aws-and-nvidia-announce-second-cohort-of-physical-ai-fellowship/) with **AWS** and **NVIDIA**.

The part that still feels magical is robot learning: watching a robot learn a behaviour instead of hand-coding every step. I've worked with classical control, like high-frequency impedance control, and with **diffusion policies**, **vision-language-action models**, and **world action models**. My honest view is that the best systems sit somewhere in the middle. Robots need learning, but they also need structure, safety, feedback, and respect for physics.

Nobody really knows the full recipe yet. We still don't know how to deploy general-purpose robots at scale that do genuinely useful work, and that's exactly what makes it worth doing. Robots are where AI finally gets tested against reality, and the question I keep coming back to is simple: **what actually works at scale?**
